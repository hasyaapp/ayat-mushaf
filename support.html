Tajwid Warna support

Found a letter with the wrong color, a mistake in a verse or a translation, or need help? Email asharyhsm@gmail.com. Please include the surah and verse numbers and, if possible, a screenshot.

Questions

  • Where do the tajwid rules come from? From the QPC Hafs Tajweed data of the Quranic Universal Library (riwayah Hafs 'an 'Asim). The app matches each marked letter to the Madinah mushaf script it draws. Check printed teaching material with your teacher, and tell us about any letter that looks wrong.
  • How do I color only one lesson? Open "Tajwid rules to color" and pick a group, such as "Nun sakinah and tanwin", or a single rule. Letters of the other rules keep the text color.
  • What is the legend? A line of text under the verses with a colored dot and the name of each rule that appears. It lists only the rules in the verses you chose. It is normal Canva text, so you can edit it, or turn it off before adding.
  • Why are some letters gray? Gray marks letters that are written but not read: hamzah washal, lam syamsiyah and silent alif, waw or ya. A lighter gray marks idgham mutajanisain and mutaqaribain.
  • Why is izhar not colored? Letters that are read clearly keep the text color, as in most tajwid mushafs.
  • Can I change the colors? The text color picker in the app sets the letters without a colored rule and the text below the verses. After adding, select the verses in your design to change any color from Canva's toolbar.
  • Why at most 20 verses? Longer passages become very tall and too small to read on one page. Add them in parts, for example on separate pages.
  • When is the Basmalah added? Only when you start from verse 1 of a surah other than Al-Fatihah (where it is verse 1) and At-Taubah (which has none). You can turn it off.
  • Which translation is used? The Indonesian translation by the Ministry of Religious Affairs (Kemenag RI), as published by the Quranic Universal Library. It is off by default.
  • Does it work offline? No. Verse data is downloaded when you pick a surah.
